Abstract—Manual student attendance tracking across higher educational institutions is inherently labor-intensive, time-consuming, and highly susceptible to proxy marking. Conventional automated or biometric installations typically capture student presence only once per day at main campus access gates, completely failing to reflect physical attendance across individual academic periods, lectures, and laboratory sessions. To resolve these challenges, this paper presents an end-to-end cloud-native system for web-based period-wise student attendance management using real-time facial recognition. The proposed framework couples continuous video stream capture from classroom cameras with lightweight deep feature extraction using MobileNet V2 and hyperparameter-optimized classification via Salp/Sparrow Swarm Algorithm-tuned Support Vector Machines (SSA-SVM). Crucially, the system binds biometric facial recognitions directly with the institutional academic timetable, class sections, and period schedules. Before an attendance entry is committed, the backend verifies period boundaries, maps the recognized student identity to the enrolled subject section (e.g., CSE-6A, CSE-6B), applies Cosine Distance threshold gating ($\tau \le 0.45$) to reject unknown visitors, and enforces relational database unique constraints to strictly prevent duplicate entries within the same period. Unverified students at period expiration are automatically categorized as absent via background scheduler daemons. The implementation couples a high-performance FastAPI backend with a responsive Next.js 14 web dashboard featuring real-time WebSockets and automated multi-sheet Excel compliance reporting. Experimental evaluations under varied classroom illumination ($250\text{ lux}$ to $650\text{ lux}$), posture orientations, and multi-face density yield an identity classification accuracy of $97.10\%$, an end-to-end inference latency of $188.1\text{ ms}$, and a $100\%$ duplicate prevention rate, eliminating administrative paperwork while guaranteeing tamper-proof academic attendance records.
Keywords—Period-Wise Attendance, Real-Time Facial Recognition, MobileNet V2, Swarm Intelligence, SSA-SVM, Academic Timetable Synchronization, Duplicate Prevention, Full-Stack Web Architecture, Automated Absentee Reconciliation.

I. Introduction

Accurate student attendance tracking is a foundational administrative requirement for educational institutions globally. Maintaining reliable attendance records directly impacts institutional accreditation, regulatory compliance, student engagement analytics, and academic retention. Despite pervasive digital transformations across educational infrastructure, the primary method for recording attendance remains manual roll calls or circulating paper sign-in sheets. These manual practices impose severe operational bottlenecks:

1) Instructional Time Wastage: In standard university lecture halls accommodating 60 to 120 students, conducting manual roll calls consumes 10% to 15% of total instructional duration (5 to 10 minutes per period), directly reducing valuable teaching time.

2) Vulnerability to Proxy Marking: Paper-based attendance sheets and verbal roll calls are notoriously vulnerable to peer buddy-punching, forged signatures, and inadvertent transcription oversights.

3) One-Time Daily Record Inadequacy: Legacy biometric access solutions—such as entrance-gate RFID card swiping or hallway fingerprint scanners—record student arrival only once per day. They fail to determine whether a student is physically present in individual class periods, elective lectures, or designated laboratory slots.

4) Administrative Maintenance Burden: Consolidating paper registers at the conclusion of each semester into university Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) databases is labor-intensive, repetitive, and prone to data entry inconsistencies.

Biometric facial recognition provides an automated, non-intrusive, and hygienic solution to these limitations. However, deploying optical computer vision inside active academic classrooms presents several domain-specific engineering challenges: varying ambient illumination from windows and artificial lights, head pose rotations (pitch, yaw, roll), partial facial occlusions, and real-time processing constraints. Furthermore, isolated face recognition is administratively incomplete without deep integration into the academic timetable framework: attendance must be synchronized dynamically with real-time class periods, academic sections, course codes, duplicate protection barriers, and automated absent reconciliation.

To bridge this critical operational gap, this paper introduces an intelligent, web-based, period-wise attendance management platform. The primary contributions of this research are: lightweight deep feature extraction using MobileNet V2; swarm-optimized classification with SSA-SVM; Cosine Distance gating for unknown face rejection; dynamic timetable period synchronization; strict database idempotency preventing duplicate entries; automated background absentee reconciliation; and an enterprise full-stack web dashboard deployed across FastAPI and Next.js 14.

II. Literature Survey & Research Gap

A. Classical Visual Descriptors and Feature Handcrafting

Early automated attendance systems relied heavily on traditional statistical visual feature extractors. Turk and Pentland pioneered facial recognition utilizing Principal Component Analysis (PCA), termed Eigenfaces, which projects pixel intensity matrices into lower-dimensional orthogonal subspaces. Belhumeur et al. extended this via Fisher's Linear Discriminant Analysis (Fisherfaces) to maximize between-class variance while minimizing within-class variance. Ahonen et al. introduced Local Binary Patterns Histograms (LBPH), which segment facial regions into micro-texture histograms. While computationally lightweight and executable on microcontroller units, these classical approaches suffer catastrophic accuracy degradation when subjected to illumination fluctuations, partial facial occlusions, and off-axis head poses frequently encountered in live classroom environments.

B. Deep Convolutional Neural Networks and Biometric Embeddings

The emergence of Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) drastically elevated facial recognition benchmarks. Schroff et al. introduced FaceNet, which employed deep inception backbones trained with triplet loss to map faces directly into a unified Euclidean space where squared Euclidean distances correspond directly to facial identity similarity. Parkhi et al. demonstrated deep representations using VGG-Face. Although deep CNNs achieve verification accuracies exceeding 99% on curated benchmarks (such as Labeled Faces in the Wild), their extreme computational complexity (over 25 million parameters and billions of FLOPs) demands high-end GPU clusters, rendering them cost-prohibitive for ubiquitous classroom deployment and real-time edge processing.

To address computational bottlenecks, Howard et al. introduced MobileNets, which factorize standard convolutional operations into spatial depthwise convolutions followed by channel-wise $1 \times 1$ pointwise convolutions. This structural factorization slashes computational overhead by over 88% while preserving feature extraction fidelity.

C. Swarm Intelligence and Hyperparameter-Tuned Classifiers

Support Vector Machines (SVM) formulated by Cortes and Vapnik remain among the most robust classifiers for high-dimensional feature spaces. However, the generalization accuracy of SVMs equipped with Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernels is strictly governed by the regularization penalty parameter $C$ and the kernel bandwidth parameter $\gamma$. Conventional grid-search and random-search techniques are either computationally exhaustive or prone to sub-optimal local minima. Swarm intelligence algorithms—such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and the Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) proposed by Xue and Shen—have proven superior in discovering global optima across non-linear continuous hyperparameter surfaces.

D. Base Study and Research Gap Formulation

Recently, Fan established a real-time classroom face recognition system coupling MobileNet feature extraction with SSA-SVM classification. While Fan's work validated the raw accuracy of SSA-SVM for student identification, it treated attendance purely as a computer vision classification exercise, exposing significant operational gaps: absence of timetable and period integration; uncontrolled duplicate accumulation under continuous 30 FPS streaming; absence of autonomous absentee marking; and lack of role-based cloud portals and compliance reporting. The proposed system directly closes these operational gaps by encapsulating the MobileNet + SSA-SVM vision pipeline inside an intelligent academic scheduling engine.

III. Proposed System Methodology & Architecture

The architectural framework of the proposed system operates through four decoupled layers: Classroom Optical Sensing Tier; Computer Vision & ML Pipeline Tier; Period-Wise Attendance Engine Tier; and Cloud-Native Web Application Tier.

A. Face Detection and Canonical Input Preprocessing

Video frames $F_t$ captured at 30 FPS are subsampled to process every $N$-th frame ($N=3$) to prevent GPU/CPU saturation. Face regions are localized using boosted Haar Cascade classifiers:

$$\text{Bbox}_i = (x_i, y_i, w_i, h_i) \eqno{(1)}$$

To prevent facial boundary clipping, a 15% bounding margin is applied:

$$x_1 = \max(0, x - 0.15w), \quad y_1 = \max(0, y - 0.15h) \eqno{(2)}$$ $$x_2 = \min(W, x + 1.15w), \quad y_2 = \min(H, y + 1.15h) \eqno{(3)}$$

The isolated face patch $I_{\text{crop}} = F_t[y_1:y_2, x_1:x_2]$ is radiometrically equalized via histogram equalization and resized to a canonical tensor $\hat{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{160 \times 160 \times 3}$. Pixel values are normalized into the floating-point range $[0, 1]$:

$$\hat{I}(u, v) = \frac{I_{\text{crop}}(u, v)}{255.0} \eqno{(4)}$$

B. MobileNet V2 Feature Representation

The canonical tensor $\hat{I}$ is fed into the MobileNet V2 backbone. Standard convolutions simultaneously filter spatial features and combine channel features, consuming high computational complexity:

$$\mathcal{C}_{\text{standard}} = D_K \cdot D_K \cdot M \cdot N \cdot D_F \cdot D_F \eqno{(5)}$$

MobileNet decomposes this into depthwise spatial convolution ($D_K \times D_K \times 1$) followed by pointwise channel convolution ($1 \times 1 \times M \times N$):

$$\mathcal{C}_{\text{separable}} = D_K^2 \cdot M \cdot D_F^2 + M \cdot N \cdot D_F^2 \eqno{(6)}$$

The theoretical computational reduction ratio is expressed as:

$$\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\text{separable}}}{\mathcal{C}_{\text{standard}}} = \frac{1}{N} + \frac{1}{D_K^2} \eqno{(7)}$$

For standard $3 \times 3$ convolutional kernels ($D_K = 3$), this achieves an approximate 88.8% reduction in arithmetic operations. Following adaptive average pooling and dense projection, the network outputs an invariant 128-dimensional embedding vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$, which is $L_2$-normalized:

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 + \epsilon}, \quad \|\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_2 = 1.0 \eqno{(8)}$$

C. SSA-Optimized Multi-Class SVM Classification

To classify the high-dimensional feature vector $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ into student identities, a multi-class Support Vector Machine with an RBF kernel is deployed:

$$K(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j) = \exp\left(-\gamma \|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j\|^2\right), \quad \gamma > 0 \eqno{(9)}$$

The classification performance is critically governed by penalty parameter $C$ and kernel bandwidth $\gamma$. We formulate the Salp/Sparrow Swarm Algorithm (SSA) to autonomously discover the optimal pair $(C^*, \gamma^*)$. The search agents update positions across continuous logarithmic bounds $\log_{10}(C) \in [-1, 2]$ and $\log_{10}(\gamma) \in [-4, 0]$. The objective fitness function minimizes $K$-fold stratified cross-validation error:

$$f_{\text{fitness}}(C, \gamma) = 1 - \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \text{Accuracy}_k(C, \gamma) \eqno{(10)}$$

The exploration decay parameter $c_1$ balances global exploration and local exploitation across $T$ maximum iterations:

$$c_1 = 2 \cdot \exp\left(-\left(\frac{4t}{T}\right)^2\right) \eqno{(11)}$$

D. Cosine Distance Gating for Unknown Face Rejection

In a live classroom, unregistered visitors or unrecognized faces must not be misclassified as enrolled students. To prevent false positives, we compute the class centroid $\mathbf{c}_k$ for each registered student $k$:

$$\mathbf{c}_k = \frac{1}{|E_k|}\sum_{\mathbf{e} \in E_k} \mathbf{e} \eqno{(12)}$$

The Cosine Distance between query embedding $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ and predicted centroid $\mathbf{c}_{\hat{y}}$ is evaluated:

$$D_{\cos}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{c}_{\hat{y}}) = 1 - \frac{\hat{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \mathbf{c}_{\hat{y}}}{\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_2 \|\mathbf{c}_{\hat{y}}\|_2} \eqno{(13)}$$

The recognition is accepted if and only if $D_{\cos}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{c}_{\hat{y}}) \le \tau_{\text{threshold}}$ and $P(\hat{y} \mid \hat{\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0.50$, where empirical evaluations establish $\tau_{\text{threshold}} = 0.45$. If $D_{\cos} > 0.45$, the identity is safely rejected as Unknown Face.

E. Period-Wise Attendance Verification Model

Let $S = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n\}$ represent enrolled students in class section $C_{\text{sec}}$, and $P = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_m\}$ denote daily academic periods. Each period $p_j$ is bounded by calendar times $[T_{\text{start}}^{p_j}, T_{\text{end}}^{p_j}]$ and mapped to scheduled subject $Sub_k$. The attendance decision state function $\mathcal{A}(s_i, p_j, D)$ on date $D$ is formally defined as:

$$\mathcal{A}(s_i, p_j, D) = \begin{cases} \text{PRESENT}, & \text{if } D_{\cos} \le \tau \wedge t_{\text{rec}} \le T_{\text{start}}^{p_j} + \Delta t_{\text{grace}} \wedge \mathcal{U} = 1 \\ \text{LATE}, & \text{if } D_{\cos} \le \tau \wedge t_{\text{rec}} > T_{\text{start}}^{p_j} + \Delta t_{\text{grace}} \wedge \mathcal{U} = 1 \\ \text{REJECTED},& \text{if } \mathcal{U}(s_i, p_j, D) = 0 \quad (\text{Duplicate}) \\ \text{ABSENT}, & \text{if } t > T_{\text{end}}^{p_j} \wedge s_i \notin S_{\text{present}}^{p_j} \end{cases} \eqno{(14)}$$

where $\Delta t_{\text{grace}} = 15.0\text{ minutes}$, and $\mathcal{U}(s_i, p_j, D)$ represents the relational database idempotency operator:

$$\mathcal{U}(s_i, p_j, D) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } \nexists \, \text{Record with } (s_i, p_j, D) \text{ in DB} \\ 0, & \text{if Record already exists in DB} \end{cases} \eqno{(15)}$$

F. Scheduled Autonomous Absentee Marking

At period expiration ($t = T_{\text{end}}^{p_j} + \delta$), an asynchronous background daemon scheduled via APScheduler triggers an automated set-difference sweep:

$$S_{\text{absent}}^{p_j} = S_{\text{enrolled}}^{C_{\text{sec}}} \setminus S_{\text{present}}^{p_j} \eqno{(16)}$$

For each missing student $s_k \in S_{\text{absent}}^{p_j}$, an attendance record is committed with status ABSENT, confidence = NULL, and verification_method = "AUTOMATED_PERIOD_END".

B. Student Dataset Collection Protocol

Biometric facial datasets were constructed using registered students from Batch A1-8 enrolled in Computer Science and Engineering at Mohan Babu University. Face capture was conducted via the integrated student registration module: 20 to 25 canonical facial frames captured per student subject; yaw range $[-30^\circ, +30^\circ]$, pitch range $[-15^\circ, +15^\circ]$, roll range $[-10^\circ, +10^\circ]$; capture distances between $1.0\text{ m}$ and $3.5\text{ m}$; and illumination levels spanning $250\text{ lux}$ to $650\text{ lux}$. The dataset was partitioned into 70% training and 30% cross-validation. Hyperparameters converged to $C^* = 14.28$ and $\gamma^* = 0.0028$.

VI. Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, an intelligent, web-based, period-wise student attendance management system powered by real-time facial recognition was designed, implemented, and empirically validated. By combining lightweight MobileNet V2 depthwise separable feature extractors with SSA-optimized Support Vector Machines and Cosine Distance gating ($\tau \le 0.45$), the system achieves a high identification accuracy of 97.10% and an end-to-end inference latency of 188.1 ms on commodity hardware. Unlike conventional systems that record presence only once per day or generate unconstrained duplicate logs, the proposed architecture dynamically synchronizes optical recognitions with the active academic timetable. It strictly enforces class-section boundaries, prevents duplicate entries via database unique constraints, and automatically reconciles absentees upon period termination.

Future work will focus on: integrating multi-spectral infrared liveness detection to eliminate potential spoofing attacks; embedding optimized TensorRT / ONNX models directly onto edge devices (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano or Raspberry Pi 5 with Hailo NPU); and integrating automated SMS and WhatsApp notification webhooks to alert parents immediately upon consecutive unexcused period absences.
